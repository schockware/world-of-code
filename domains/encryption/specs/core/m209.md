# ENC-M209: M-209 (core)

Covers `m209`, the US Army Converter M-209 (Hagelin C-38). Six pin wheels and a 27-bar lug cage produce a shift `K` for each letter, and the output is a reversed alphabet shifted by `K`, so the same settings encipher and decipher.

Only the converter is specified. The caller supplies the start positions directly. These are operator or display concerns, and are not modeled:
- **Indicators:** deriving the internal message indicator from the external one and the system letter.
- **Letter counter.**
- **The C/D switch:** five-letter groups in C mode, and printing Z as a space in D mode.

Fixed hardware data (wheel alphabets and sensed-pin offsets) is vendored in `../../standards/m209/m209.json`. Research is in `../../docs/m209.md` and `../../docs/m209-wiring-data.md`. Letters are numbered A=0 to Z=25 (ENC-TEXT-003).

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Wheel 1-6** | The key wheels, left to right, with 26, 25, 23, 21, 19 and 17 letters. Each has its own alphabet: wheel 2 has no W, wheel 3 has no W, Y or Z, and wheels 4-6 end at U, S and Q. |
| **Window letter** | The letter showing on a wheel. `positions` gives the six window letters at the start. |
| **Sensed pin** | The pin being read on a wheel: the letter a fixed number of places after the window letter, in that wheel's own alphabet (15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10 for wheels 1-6, so `AAAAAA` senses `PONMLK`). |
| **Active wheel** | A wheel whose sensed pin is effective, meaning its letter is in that wheel's `pins`. |
| **Bar** | One of the 27 cage bars. Its two lugs each face a wheel (1-6) or a neutral slot (0). |
| **K** | The number of bars, 0 to 27, with at least one lug facing an active wheel. |

## Requirements

### ENC-M209-001
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST
`pins` MUST hold six strings, one per wheel, each listing that wheel's effective pins in any order, and possibly empty. A string with anything other than capital letters MUST be rejected with reason `key.not-letters`. A letter that is not on its wheel MUST be rejected with reason `key.not-on-wheel`. A letter listed twice for one wheel MUST be rejected with reason `key.repeated-pin`.
**Rationale:** Sets of effective letters are how every printed key list writes pins. A pin that does not exist cannot be set, and a duplicate means the key was mistyped.
**Contract:** `keys/m209.json`. The repeated-pin rule is not expressible in the schema.
**Vectors:** `m209.json#reject-pin*`, `m209.json#reject-repeated-pin`, `m209.json#accept-empty-pins-neutral-lugs`

### ENC-M209-002
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST
`lugs` MUST hold exactly 27 bars, each written `a-b` with single digits. A digit above 6 MUST be rejected with reason `key.out-of-range`. A bar with both lugs facing the same wheel (`3-3`) MUST be rejected with reason `key.lug-conflict`. `0-0` is a valid bar with no effect. The order of the bars, and of the two digits in a bar, MUST NOT change the result.
**Rationale:** The cage has 27 bars and every key list sets all of them. A bar has one slot per wheel, so two lugs cannot share one. The cage only counts displaced bars, so order is irrelevant.
**Contract:** `keys/m209.json`
**Vectors:** `m209.json#reject-lug-*`, `m209.json#bar-and-lug-order-irrelevant`, `m209.json#accept-empty-pins-neutral-lugs`

### ENC-M209-003
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST
`positions` MUST be exactly six capital letters, or else be rejected with reason `key.not-letters`. Each letter MUST be on its wheel, or else be rejected with reason `key.not-on-wheel`.
**Rationale:** A wheel can only be turned to a letter it carries. Position W on wheel 2 does not exist.
**Contract:** `keys/m209.json`
**Vectors:** `m209.json#reject-position*`, `m209.json#accept-last-letter-each-wheel`

### ENC-M209-004
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST
For each letter, each wheel's sensed pin MUST be the letter its offset places after the window letter in that wheel's own alphabet, wrapping at the end. After each letter, every wheel MUST advance by one letter in its own alphabet, wrapping at the end.
**Rationale:** All six wheels advance together, with no carry, so the key stream repeats only after 26 x 25 x 23 x 21 x 19 x 17 = 101,405,850 letters. The skipped letters mean V is followed by X on wheels 2 and 3, and X by A on wheel 3.
**Contract:** `../../standards/m209/m209.json`
**Vectors:** `m209.json#wheel-2-skips-w`, `m209.json#wheel-3-wraps-x-to-a`, `m209.json#sensed-pin-is-offset`, `m209.json#published-check-*`

### ENC-M209-005
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST
For each letter, `K` MUST be the number of bars with at least one lug facing an active wheel. A bar MUST be counted at most once, even when both its lugs face active wheels.
**Rationale:** Each displaced bar moves the print wheel one place, and a bar is either displaced or not.
**Contract:** `keys/m209.json`
**Vectors:** `m209.json#bar-counts-once`, `m209.json#no-pins-is-atbash`, `m209.json#all-bars-fire-k-27-folds`, `m209.json#published-check-*`

### ENC-M209-006
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST
Each letter MUST be enciphered as `C = (K - 1 - P) mod 26`. `K` of 26 and 27 therefore act as 0 and 1.
**Rationale:** The print wheel is the reversed alphabet shifted by `K`. All four published 26-letter checks reproduce with this formula and with no other offset. The README's `C = K - P` holds only with plaintext numbered from A=1, and with A=0 throughout it is `K - 1 - P`.
**Contract:** None. This is behavior.
**Vectors:** `m209.json#published-check-*`, `m209.json#no-pins-is-atbash`, `m209.json#all-bars-fire-k-27-folds`

### ENC-M209-007
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST
Each letter of the text, Z included, MUST advance the machine once and produce exactly one letter.
**Rationale:** The machine enciphers every letter keyed, and Z is the letter operators keyed for a space. Treating Z as a space is printing, not enciphering.
**Contract:** None. This is behavior.
**Vectors:** `m209.json#published-fm-*`, `m209.json#published-indicator-tape`, `m209.json#aa-start-ygxrel`, `m209.json#fm-pangram`, `m209.json#published-check-*`

### ENC-M209-008
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST
`encrypt` and `decrypt` MUST be the same operation: for the same key, both MUST give the same output for the same input.
**Rationale:** The formula is its own inverse (`P = (K - 1 - C) mod 26`), and the C/D switch changed only the printing, not the key stream.
**Contract:** None. This is behavior.
**Vectors:** `m209.json#published-fm-mission-accomplished`, `m209.json#fm-attack-at-dawn-decrypt`, `m209.json#fm-pangram-decrypt-same-operation`

### ENC-M209-009
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST NOT
An implementation MUST NOT prevent a letter from enciphering to itself.
**Rationale:** Unlike Enigma, the M-209 enciphers P to itself whenever `K = 2P + 1 (mod 26)`. An implementation that borrowed Enigma's rule would be wrong.
**Contract:** None. This is behavior.
**Vectors:** `m209.json#all-bars-fire-k-27-folds` (A to A), `m209.json#sensed-pin-is-offset`

### ENC-M209-010
**Status:** Draft
**Level:** MUST
When a key has more than one fault, the reason MUST be from the first check that fails, in this order, each check applied to every wheel or bar before the next: the shape (`key.malformed`); pin letters (`key.not-letters`); pins on their wheels (`key.not-on-wheel`); repeated pins (`key.repeated-pin`); lug digits (`key.out-of-range`); lug conflicts (`key.lug-conflict`); `positions` letters (`key.not-letters`); positions on their wheels (`key.not-on-wheel`).
**Rationale:** One predictable reason per key, in the order a key list is set: pins, then lugs, then the wheels turned to the message indicator.
**Contract:** None. This is behavior.
**Vectors:** `m209.json#precedence-*`

## Notes

- **Published vectors.** The four 26-letter checks (key lists FM, AA, YL, AB) encipher `A` 26 times from `AAAAAA`. They are how operators verified a key list was set correctly, and the check letters read off `K` directly. `published-fm-attack-at-dawn`, `published-fm-mission-accomplished` and `published-indicator-tape` come from Blair's procedure write-up and the `m209` library tests. The research reproduced them, and so did both throwaway implementations here. The library and Blair are third-party sources, and no US Army primary example was found.
- **The mission-accomplished vector shows the Z rule.** Its published plaintext reads `NEUTRALI ED X  ERO CASUALTIES`. The machine printed every Z as a space in decipher mode, so the real Zs in NEUTRALIZED and ZERO came out as gaps. The domain returns the Zs and leaves the decision to the reader.
- **Z is a decoding problem, not a cipher problem.** A Z in decrypted text may be a space or a real Z, and nothing in the ciphertext says which. Operators resolved it from their own experience, reading `NEUTRALI ED` as NEUTRALIZED. It is the same ambiguity as an X separator (`msg.md`), and it belongs to whoever turns letters back into words.
- **The final `O`** is the decryption of the sender's padding `X`.
- **The indicator-tape vector is ordinary encryption.** Enciphering the system letter 12 times at the external indicator gives the tape from which the operator picks the internal indicator. The picking (skipping letters a wheel lacks) is the operator procedure.
- **Keyspace.** 2^131 pin settings, and about 2.2 x 10^13 distinct lug cages by effect, for about 6 x 10^52 together (`../../docs/m209.md`, section 5). Hill climbing recovers the settings from about 500 letters of ciphertext alone.
- **Computed vectors.** `aa-start-ygxrel` and `fm-pangram` were computed-only in the research. They, and every other unpublished vector here, agree between two independent implementations, one of which reads `../../standards/m209/m209.json`.

## Population assumptions

This spec assumes the operator or population handles the following.

- **Z for space.** Keying Z between words, spelling out numbers, and using phonetic words for single letters. The mechanical part is specified by the `m209-army` operator (`operator/opm209.md`).
- **Reading Z back.** Deciding which decrypted Zs were spaces and which were letters, as operators did by experience. The domain never guesses.
- **Printing.** Five-letter groups when enciphering, padding the last group (the `m209` library pads with X), and printing Z as a space when deciphering.
- **Key lists.** Expanding the shorthand `2-0*8` into eight bars, and reading pin grids into letter sets.
- **Indicators.** Choosing the external indicator and system letter, deriving the internal indicator from the 12-letter tape, and framing the message with indicator groups (`GGABC DEFFM ... GGABC DEFFM`).
- **Letter counter.** Resetting and reading it is operator procedure. The domain has no counter.
